words; holds and repetitions follow the sources. No images from these pages are bundled. The app credits

Visual Aids Centre is clear that these ease eye strain and don't change eyesight, so the app doesn't promise
better vision. Not adapted from it: pencil push-ups (closer to a therapy exercise than a break), and
"zooming" and "refocusing breaks", which repeat near-far focus and the 20-20-20 rule.

## From a doctor

Three eye exercises come from an exercise sheet the user was given by their own doctor. There's no public page
to link, so the app credits them as "a doctor's exercise sheet" without naming the doctor. The wording stays
close to the original, with the doctor's holds and directions.

| Exercise (key) | Notes |
|---|---|
| Open eye rotations (`eye_rotations`) | "Trying to move the eyes farther" softened to "a little further without straining" |
| Eye-head rotations (`eye_head_rotations`) | |
| Head swings (`head_swings`) | |

Not adapted: Wellness52's neck extension release (5–10 minutes lying on a rolled towel, with a caution about
lightheadedness), which is too long and too cautious a move for a quick break.

Also consulted, but nothing taken: [We Are Knitters: hand exercises for knitters](https://blog.weareknitters.com/knitters-life/hand-exercises-for-knitters/)
and [Pink Sheep Design: stretches for crafters](https://therealpinksheepdesign.com/stretches-for-crafters-makers-artists-that-will-help-you-feel-much-better/).
Both present their exercises only as images, with no written steps to adapt.

The safety line shown with every exercise follows both sources' advice that a mild pull is fine and pain
means stop.
